<section>
 <details class="guide" id="export">
  <summary>How to export your Google search history</summary>
  <p class="mute" style="margin-top:12px">Takes about 5 minutes of clicking, plus a short wait. Use a computer if you can; the steps are easier there.</p>
  <ol>
   <li>Go to <a href="https://takeout.google.com" rel="noopener">takeout.google.com</a> and sign in to your Google account.</li>
   <li>Click <strong>Deselect all</strong>.</li>
   <li>Scroll down and tick <strong>My Activity</strong>.</li>
   <li>Click <strong>All activity data included</strong>, then <strong>Deselect all</strong>, tick only <strong>Search</strong>, and press OK.</li>
   <li>Click the format button (it reads something like <strong>Multiple formats</strong> or "HTML and 2 other formats") and change <strong>Activity records</strong> from HTML to <strong>JSON</strong>. Press OK.</li>
   <li>Scroll to the bottom, click <strong>Next step</strong>, keep the default settings (export once, .zip), then click <strong>Create export</strong>.</li>
   <li>Wait for Google's email, "Your Google data is ready to be downloaded". It's often within about 20 minutes but can take longer. Download the .zip and open it.</li>
   <li>Open <strong>Takeout &rsaquo; My Activity &rsaquo; Search &rsaquo; MyActivity.json</strong>. Upload that file to Emotion, or open it in a text editor and paste everything.</li>
  </ol>
  <p class="warn"><strong>Choose JSON, not HTML.</strong> Emotion can't read the default HTML format.</p>
  <p class="mute" style="margin-top:14px;font-size:15px">Your export covers all your history, but Emotion only looks at the last six months. The file is personal, so don't send it to anyone, and delete it when you're done. Google sometimes renames menu items; if a label looks different, the steps are the same.</p>
 </details>
